We build organs on chips to study microbes, metabolism and cancer

Our microfluidic organ-on-chips and biosensors model disease and measure living biology, bringing together engineering, biology and computation.

Led by Abhinav Bhushan, Associate Professor of Biomedical Engineering at Illinois Institute of Technology.

What we study

All research projects

Microbes and colorectal cancer

Colon organoids and chips that host living bacteria, to test how bile acids and microbial metabolites change tumor epithelium.

Colon and microbiome projects

Liver–fat crosstalk

Linked adipose and liver tissues on chip for studying insulin resistance and fatty liver disease in human cells.

Liver and adipose projects

Microbiome and drug response

Oxygen-controlled intestine chips and pharmacokinetic models that show how gut bacteria alter drug absorption and metabolism.

Drug response projects

Sensing, control and computation

Electrochemical and breath sensors for continuous measurement, closed-loop control of cultures, and machine learning on host–microbe data.

Sensing and computation projects
